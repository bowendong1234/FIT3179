In 2023, Australian bushfires were particularly prevalent in the Northern Territory and parts of Western Australia. 2023 saw significant fire activity driven by the build-up of dry fuel after a wet season. In the Northern Territory alone, around 43 million hectares were affected with large fires occurring in remote savannah and desert regions. These fires were primarily fueled by lightning strikes and eggravated by the vast, dry grasslands created after heavy rains earlier in the year. The Great Sandy Desert and Barkly regions were the most highly affected with fires burning through large tracts of unmanaged land.
In New South Wales, numerous fires also broke out in October 2023, with the Mid North Coast and areas around Kempsey experiencing significant fire outbreaks. Fires such as the Willi Willi Road blaze in the Willi Willi National Park grew rapidly, burning over 30,000 hectares. Extreme conditions, including high winds and soaring temperatures, contributed to the rapid spread of fires, which forced evacuations and led to property losses across various parts of the state, including Penrith, Nymboida, and Tenterfield.
The 2019-2020 bushfires in Australia, known as the Black Summer bushfires were some of the most devastating bushfires in Australia's recent history. Officially beginning in June 2019 and ending in around May 2020, these bushfires claimed the lives of over 33 individuals and impacted multiple states but particularly New South Wales, Victoria, and Queensland. The fires were driven by extreme weather conditions, including prolonged drought, record-high temperatures, and strong winds, which contributed to the intensity and spread of the blazes. When Black Summer ended, over 24 million hectares of land was burned.
The line charts to the right display some of the secondary effects of the Black Summer bushfires. The line chart on top visualises the rate of change in the number of PBS (Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme) scripts of inhalants and adrenergics and the line chart on the bottom visualises the rate of change in the number of sales for inhalers for shortness of breath. The yellow shaded region is the time period in which the most devastating fires of the Black Summer period occurred and the dotted line represents the peak in the number of scripts ordered and sales. From these charts, it is evident that during the time when the most devastating fires of Black Summer were burning, the prescriptions for inhalents and adrenergics steeply increased. This was due to the poor air quality caused by the large amounts of ash that was spread into the atmosphere by wind which ultimately caused respiratory issues for people not only in bush fire affected areas but also people in non bush fire affected areas. The same can be said for the sharp increase in sales for inhalers as the hazardous air quality worsened symptoms for people with respiratory conditions such as asthma.
The chart below shows the number of deaths caused by the Black Summer bushfires per state. The majority of these fatalities occurred in New South Wales which was the state hardest hit by the fires. Particularly severe fires in areas such as the South Coast and the Blue Mountains accounted for many of the deaths. One of the deadliest single incidents was the Currowan fire, which claimed multiple lives and destroyed over 500 homes. Many of the deaths were directly linked to the intensity of the fires, which were fueled by extreme weather conditions, including prolonged drought, record-breaking temperatures, and high winds. These conditions created ideal environments for firestorms, where the combination of heat and wind accelerates the spread of the flames, often leaving little time for evacuation.

Forest fires in Australia during the 2019-2020 period heavily affected New South Wales (NSW) and the Australian Capital Territory (ACT), alongside other states. NSW, in particular, experienced a series of devastating forest fires throughout the year, not limited to the Black Summer peak. These fires were driven by record-breaking temperatures, low humidity, and prolonged drought conditions, which created highly flammable environments in forested regions. In NSW, significant forest fires began as early as September 2019, with areas such as the Mid North Coast and Blue Mountains being severely impacted. Fires burned through millions of hectares of forest, including World Heritage-listed areas, such as the Gondwana Rainforests. These forest ecosystems, normally resistant to fire, were overwhelmed by the extreme conditions. By the end of the season, over 5 million hectares had been scorched in NSW alone, making it one of the worst-affected states.
The ACT, though much smaller in land area, was also severely impacted, particularly by fires in early 2020. The Orroral Valley fire in Namadgi National Park, which started in January 2020, burned over 80% of the parks forested areas, threatening Canberra's outskirts. The fires in the ACT emphasized the vulnerability of forests in small, densely populated regions during such intense fire seasons.
